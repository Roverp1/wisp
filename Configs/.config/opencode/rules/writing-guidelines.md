# Writing guidelines

Follow project-specific instructions, established workflows,
and document templates. When they require particular content,
structure, or detail, preserve those requirements (project's specific requirements can overwrite general rules).

## General

- Be direct, concise, clear, and specific.
- Lead with the answer, decision, or actionable information.
- Use plain language, short sentences, and active voice.
- Prefer concrete statements over vague or abstract descriptions.
- Remove filler, repetition, unnecessary qualifiers, and generic introductions or conclusions.
- Avoid marketing language, exaggerated claims, artificial enthusiasm, and rhetorical flourishes.
- Do not narrate your process or explain obvious actions unless requested.
- Avoid restating the user's request or repeating information already established.
- Use headings, lists, and tables only when they improve readability.
- Do not sacrifice important details, accuracy, or clarity for brevity.

## Technical documentation

- Follow the project's existing documentation architecture, conventions, and templates.
- Write for developers who need to use, understand, or maintain the software.
- Identify the intended reader and purpose before writing.
- Follow Diátaxis principles: distinguish tutorials, how-to guides, reference documentation, and explanations. Use the structure appropriate to the task.
- Prioritize practical usage, interfaces, configuration, constraints, and non-obvious behavior.
- Start with the information the reader needs most.
- Prefer concrete examples and instructions over lengthy descriptions.
- Document why something works a certain way when that reasoning matters. Avoid repeating what is obvious from the code.
- Do not include implementation history, design rationale, or architectural overviews unless relevant to the reader.
- Avoid boilerplate sections and headings that add no value.
- Keep terminology consistent and descriptions precise.
- Preserve necessary prerequisites, warnings, limitations, and edge cases.
- Link to existing documentation instead of duplicating it when practical.

## Code comments

- Explain intent, non-obvious behavior, and important constraints.
- Do not paraphrase what the code already clearly expresses.
- Keep comments concise and accurate.

## Final review

Before delivering written content:

- Remove sentences and sections that add no useful information.
- Eliminate redundant explanations and unnecessary narrative.
- Ensure the result remains clear, complete, and actionable.
